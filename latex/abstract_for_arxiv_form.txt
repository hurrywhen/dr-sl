Cloud-local LLM inference forwards a de-identified artifact to a cloud model only after a local release gate approves it. We audit whether such approval can be trusted, studying one runtime-sensitive-set (runtime-S) release chain on frozen synthetic benchmarks, scoped to this implementation rather than other gates or real deployments. Three core findings: (1) missed extraction causes false release — the chain releases 57.5% of a shared-vocab holdout, yet 78.3% of released instances (Wilson [64.4,87.7]) retain gold sensitive literals, banking 20/20 all leaking; (2) the utility gate rewards unknown task coverage — with no task attribute extracted, utility falls back to unmarked-context retention, so worse extraction makes release easier (65% of releases pass through this harmful fallback, all leaking); a coverage gate leaves 5/80 releases with 0 observed false releases (Wilson upper bound 43.4%) — too few to validate safety; (3) the closed loop adds no benefit over one-shot deterministic generalization — paired release and leakage differences are zero on three holdouts, local utility is lower for the loop on all three, and downstream reference-answer preservation shows no detectable mean difference. A fully-disjoint-vocabulary stress test and dose-response series show recall collapsing with overlap; fixing clear defects removes observed false release on shared vocabulary (0/46) but not under shift. A diagnostic cloud-assisted neural extractor keeps recall 0.965 under full displacement; residual false release there traces entirely to three types omitted from the frozen taxonomy, and completing the taxonomy on a new holdout removes that gap (0/32) but one within-taxonomy miss persists (1/32): the gate inherits even neural misses. We contribute a reproducible audit framework with conditional false-release metrics, stratification, and public raw outputs, not certified zero leakage.
