Cloud-local LLM inference forwards a de-identified artifact to a cloud model only after a local release gate approves it. We audit whether such approval is trustworthy, studying a runtime-sensitive-set (runtime-S) release chain combining deterministic extraction, truthful generalization, local attack/utility checks, all-occurrence scrubbing, and human fallback. On a frozen synthetic benchmark, a disjoint-vocabulary stress test, controlled-disclosure LLM audits, and downstream-task evaluation, we report four findings. (1) Local release is systematically misaligned with external leakage: the chain releases 57.5% of a shared-vocabulary holdout yet 78.3% of released instances retain gold sensitive literals; under vocabulary shift it releases 93.75% and every released instance leaks. (2) A closed loop gives no advantage over one-shot deterministic generalization: paired release and leakage differences are zero while local utility is lower. (3) The local utility surrogate is poorly calibrated against an offline semantic judge, and whole-set averages mask the released subgroup's higher leakage and utility. (4) Residual reports cover none of the true residual risk, because the dangerous leftovers are exactly the attributes the extractor never found. We contribute a reproducible release-audit framework with a conditional false-release metric, release-stratified and domain-stratified risk analysis, corrected audit-coverage counting, calibration and residual-report analyses, and public raw audit outputs. The evidence supports an auditable workflow and a clear failure-mode account, not a certified zero-leakage or superior automatic sanitizer.
