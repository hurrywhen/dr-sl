Cloud-local LLM inference must keep sensitive data on-device while using cloud reasoning. We revise DR-SL as an auditable release chain combining runtime sensitive-set extraction, truthful generalization, local attack/utility checks, deterministic all-occurrence scrubbing, and human fallback. Repeated probe copies are not independent trials; gold annotations are not runtime inputs; online semantic judges are offline criteria; external re-testing is controlled disclosure, not pre-egress prevention. We remove an invalid Fano threshold corollary, keep feasibility statements in mutual-information units, and implement the declared stability window. On a frozen 80-instance synthetic holdout, a local deterministic runtime-S track releases 57.5% under a heuristic gate, but external gold-literal auditing finds residual exposure (mean gold hit rate 0.121) because extraction recall is 0.858. A controlled-disclosure LLM audit on 40 instances confirms residual hits (external max hit rate 0.130) and low semantic utility (0.363); the local utility surrogate correlates weakly with the offline judge (rho=0.326). Local release is not zero-leakage evidence. Oracle-S experiments are upper-bound references. Human-study reanalysis shows overall utility correlation rho=0.839 but lower within-group correlations, and the original combination-risk annotation criterion failed (kappa=-0.083); construct-aligned kappa=0.696 is post hoc. Self-learning remains exploratory. The contribution is an auditable workflow and failure-mode account, not certified zero leakage or full automation.
